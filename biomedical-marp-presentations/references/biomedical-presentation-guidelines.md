# Biomedical Presentation Guidelines

<structure_principles>
<opening>
10-15% of slides:
- Title slide with full citation info
- Learning objectives or outline
- Context/background (brief)
</opening>

<body>
70-80% of slides:
- Organized by key concepts
- Mix of concise and detailed explanatory slides
- Regular visual reinforcement
</body>

<closing>
10% of slides:
- Summary/take-home messages
- Clinical implications
- References/bibliography
</closing>
</structure_principles>

<slide_type_patterns>
<concept_introduction>
**Type**: Concise slides

**Structure**:
- Title + 3-5 bullet points
- One supporting image
- Minimal text
</concept_introduction>

<explanatory_slides>
**When to use**: Complex mechanisms, controversial topics, foundational concepts

**Structure**:
- Clear heading
- 1-2 paragraphs (150-250 words)
- Supporting diagram or figure
- Key point highlighted

**Frequency**: Every 4-6 slides
</explanatory_slides>

<visual_evidence>
**Structure**:
- Large, high-quality figure
- Minimal text
- Citation at bottom
- Brief interpretation
</visual_evidence>

<comparison_slides>
**Structure**:
- Side-by-side layout
- Tables or paired images
- Clear labels
- Highlight differences
</comparison_slides>

<clinical_application>
**Structure**:
- Case-based format
- Real-world context
- Evidence-based recommendations
</clinical_application>
</slide_type_patterns>

<scientific_accuracy_standards>
<evidence_hierarchy>
1. Meta-analyses and systematic reviews
2. Randomized controlled trials
3. Cohort studies
4. Case-control studies
5. Case series/reports
6. Expert opinion
</evidence_hierarchy>

<citation_requirements>
- Every factual claim needs a source
- Prefer primary sources when possible
- Include publication year
- Use numbered citation system for MARP presentations
</citation_requirements>

<image_attribution>
- Source must be clear (journal, author, year)
- Permission status noted if necessary
- Original figure caption preserved
</image_attribution>
</scientific_accuracy_standards>

<visual_design_principles>
<color_usage>
- **Clinical education**: Blues and greens (professional, calming)
- **Research presentations**: Dark backgrounds with high contrast
- **General biomedical**: Light backgrounds with dark text
- **Consistency**: Use same color palette throughout
</color_usage>

<typography>
- Title: 32-40pt
- Body text: 20-24pt
- Captions: 16-18pt
- Never below 14pt
</typography>

<image_quality>
- Minimum 300 DPI for photos
- Vector graphics when possible
- Proper resolution for slide dimensions
</image_quality>

<white_space>
- Avoid cluttered slides
- Use padding and margins
- One main idea per slide
</white_space>
</visual_design_principles>

<content_density_guidelines>
<high_level_slides>
**Concise format**:
- 20-40 words
- 1 image optional
- Large text
- Quick comprehension
</high_level_slides>

<mid_level_slides>
**Standard format**:
- 50-100 words
- 1-2 images
- Balance text and visuals
- Standard pace
</mid_level_slides>

<explanatory_slides>
**Detailed format**:
- 150-250 words
- 1 supporting visual
- Paragraph format
- Slower pace, deeper understanding
</explanatory_slides>

<ratio>
Aim for 70% high/mid-level, 30% explanatory slides
</ratio>
</content_density_guidelines>

- Mechanisms of action
- Temporal sequences
- Hierarchical relationships
</when_to_use>

<pubmed_integration>
<search_strategy>
1. Use Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms
2. Combine with AND/OR operators
3. Filter by publication type (meta-analysis, RCT, review)
4. Limit to recent publications (last 5-10 years for current topics)
5. Check citation count for impact
</search_strategy>

<information_extraction>
- Key findings from abstracts
- Methodology (sample size, design)
- Statistical significance (p-values, confidence intervals)
- Clinical relevance
- Limitations
</information_extraction>

<citation_format>
**Numbered system** (preferred for MARP):
```
Key finding here.[1]

References:
1. Author et al. Journal. Year;Volume:Pages. PMID: 12345678
```

**Author-year system** (alternative):
```
Key finding here (Smith et al., 2023).
```
</citation_format>
</pubmed_integration>

<accessibility_considerations>
- High contrast text/background
- Avoid red-green only distinctions (colorblind)
- Alt text for images in speaker notes
- Clear font (sans-serif preferred)
- Logical reading order
</accessibility_considerations>

<time_allocation>
<one_hour_presentation>
**For ~30-40 slides**:
- Opening: 3-5 slides
- Body: 25-30 slides
- Summary: 2-3 slides
- References: 1-2 slides
</one_hour_presentation>

<pacing>
- Simple slides: 30-45 seconds
- Standard slides: 1-2 minutes
- Explanatory slides: 3-4 minutes
- Complex diagrams: 2-3 minutes
</pacing>
</time_allocation>

<common_mistakes>
1. ❌ Too much text on one slide
2. ❌ Low-quality images
3. ❌ Missing citations
4. ❌ Inconsistent formatting
5. ❌ No explanatory depth
6. ❌ Irrelevant stock images
7. ❌ Skipping clinical relevance
8. ❌ Overcomplicated diagrams
9. ❌ Missing take-home messages
10. ❌ No clear structure
</common_mistakes>

<quality_checklist>
Before finalizing:
- [ ] Every claim has a citation
- [ ] Images are high quality and relevant
- [ ] Explanatory slides every 4-6 slides
- [ ] Consistent theme/colors
- [ ] Clear learning objectives stated
- [ ] Take-home messages summarized
- [ ] Bibliography included
- [ ] Alt text in speaker notes
- [ ] Logical flow between slides
- [ ] Clinical relevance addressed
</quality_checklist>
